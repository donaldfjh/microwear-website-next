## 2. Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ): What's Your Entry Point?

**Why it matters:** MOQ determines your initial investment and risk exposure. For new brands or market testing, lower MOQs are essential.

**Typical MOQ ranges in the industry:**

| Order Type | Standard MOQ | Price Range |
|------------|--------------|-------------|
| OEM (Only Logo) | 1,000 units | $23-$42/unit |
| OEM (full package custom design) | 3,000-5,000+ units | $18-$35/unit |
| ODM (All design) | 30K or above units | $16-$34/unit |

**What to ask:**
- What is the minimum order quantity for your standard models?
- Do you offer reduced MOQs for first-time buyers?
- Are there volume discounts for larger orders?

**Tip:** Look for manufacturers offering **MOQs as low as 100 units** for ODM products. This allows you to test market response before scaling. Microwear offers entry-level MOQs starting at 100 units for select models, making them accessible for startups and established brands alike.

## 3. Certifications: What Quality and Safety Standards Do You Meet?

**Why it matters:** Certifications are non-negotiable for market access in Europe, North America, and many other regions. Without them, your products cannot legally enter key markets.

**Essential certifications for smartwatches:**

| Certification | Region | Purpose |
|---------------|--------|---------|
| **CE** | European Union | Safety and health compliance |
| **FCC** | United States | Electromagnetic compatibility |
| **RoHS** | Global | Restriction of hazardous substances |
| **ISO 9001** | Global | Quality management systems |
| **REACH** | European Union | Chemical safety compliance |
| **Bluetooth SIG** | Global | Bluetooth interoperability |

**What to ask:**
- Which certifications do you currently hold?
- Can you provide copies of certification documents?
- Do certifications cover your entire product line?

**Red flag:** A manufacturer without CE and FCC certifications cannot legally export to Western markets. Always verify certification documents before proceeding.

**Microwear certifications:** ISO 9001, CE, FCC, RoHS, and Bluetooth SIG certified—covering all major export markets.

## 4. Production Capacity: What's Your Monthly Output?

**Why it matters:** Capacity determines whether a manufacturer can scale with your business. Underestimating demand leads to stockouts; overcommitting to a small factory risks quality issues.

**What to ask:**
- What is your current monthly production capacity?
- What percentage of capacity is currently utilized?
- Can you scale production during peak seasons?
- What is your expansion capability?

**Industry benchmarks:**
- **Small factories:** 10,000-80,000 units/month
- **Medium manufacturers:** 80,000-200,000 units/month
- **Large-scale producers:** 200,000-400,000+ units/month

**Tip:** Choose a manufacturer with **20-30% excess capacity**. This ensures they can accommodate your growth without compromising quality or lead times.

Microwear operates with a **monthly capacity of 50,000+ units**, with flexible scaling during peak demand periods.

## 5. Quality Control Process: How Do You Ensure Product Quality?

**Why it matters:** Quality defects lead to returns, warranty claims, and damaged brand reputation. A robust QC process is your insurance against these risks.

**What to ask:**
- What are the specific QC checkpoints in your production process?
- What is your defect rate?
- Do you provide QC reports with shipments?
- What is your warranty policy?

**Standard QC checkpoints:**

1. **Incoming material inspection** — Verify components meet specifications
2. **In-process inspection** — Monitor production at key stages
3. **Functional testing** — Test all features (sensors, connectivity, display)
4. **Aging test** — 24-48 hour stress test
5. **Final inspection** — AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) sampling before shipping

**Industry standards:**
- Acceptable defect rate: **<1%** (top-tier manufacturers aim for **<0.5%**)
- Warranty period: **12 months** standard

Microwear implements a **12-point QC process** for every unit, achieving a defect rate below **0.3%**—well above industry standards.

## 6. Lead Times: How Long Does Production Take?

**Why it matters:** Accurate lead time estimates are critical for inventory planning, marketing launches, and cash flow management.

**Typical lead times:**

| Order Size | OEM Lead Time | ODM Lead Time |
|------------|---------------|---------------|
| 100-500 units | 15-20 days | N/A |
| 500-1,000 units | 20-25 days | 10-12 weeks |
| 1,000-3,000 units | 25-30 days | 12-14 weeks |
| 3,000-5,000+ units | 30-35 days | 14-16 weeks |

**What to ask:**
- What is the standard production lead time for my order size?
- Does this include shipping time?
- How do you handle urgent orders?
- What are the payment terms?

**Standard payment terms:** 30% deposit upon order confirmation, 70% balance before shipping. Be wary of manufacturers demanding 100% upfront.

**Tip:** Build **2-3 weeks of buffer** into your timeline for first orders. As the relationship develops, lead times often improve.

## Red Flags to Avoid When Selecting a Manufacturer

Save yourself time and risk by watching for these warning signs:

❌ **No physical factory address** — Virtual "factories" are often trading companies with limited quality control

❌ **Prices significantly below market rate** — Usually indicates corner-cutting on materials or labor

❌ **No references or case studies** — Legitimate manufacturers have client testimonials

❌ **No certifications or expired certificates** — Non-negotiable for legal market access

❌ **Poor communication** — Slow responses now mean bigger problems later

❌ **Demanding 100% payment upfront** — Standard practice is 30% deposit
